# ORIGIN — EVIDENCE REVIEW METHODOLOGY

## Purpose

Create a transparent and reproducible method for evaluating whether context-aware evidence mapping improves biomedical research synthesis.

## 1. Define the question before searching

Write the research question, population/context, concepts, inclusion criteria and exclusion criteria before reviewing results. Record amendments with dates and reasons.

## 2. Search transparently

For each database, record:
- database and platform;
- exact search string;
- date searched;
- date limits and language limits;
- filters used;
- number of records retrieved.

Do not describe a search as systematic unless the protocol and search coverage justify that term.

## 3. Screen records

Use pre-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Record reasons for full-text exclusions. Where possible, use two independent reviewers and reconcile disagreements.

## 4. Extract evidence

For each included study, capture:
- verified bibliographic details;
- research question and design;
- population, tissue, species and disease context;
- model system and limitations;
- cell populations and cell-state definitions;
- methods and outcomes;
- key result with exact source location;
- limitations and conflicts;
- funding and disclosed conflicts of interest.

## 5. Classify evidence cautiously

Use descriptive categories such as:
- human observational;
- human interventional;
- animal in vivo;
- in vitro or ex vivo;
- computational;
- review or meta-analysis.

These labels describe study type; they do not automatically determine quality. Assess risk of bias and relevance separately.

## 6. Preserve disagreement

Do not force findings into a single consensus. Record contradictory results, differences in methods, population, timing and model systems.

## 7. Benchmark the platform

Pre-register a practical comparison:
- same research question and paper set;
- conventional review workflow versus ORIGIN-assisted workflow;
- independent reviewers;
- pre-defined outcomes such as extraction accuracy, time, agreement, missed contradictions and expert-rated usefulness.

Report failures and uncertainty. Avoid cherry-picking favorable examples.

## 8. Reproducibility package

Keep search logs, screening decisions, extraction templates, code version, data dictionary and analysis notes. Respect copyright, database terms and publisher access restrictions. Store bibliographic metadata and short factual notes rather than redistributing copyrighted full text.

## 9. Review standard

A qualified domain expert should review the scientific interpretation before it is used to support a therapeutic or investment claim.
