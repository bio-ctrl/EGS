## 1. Executive concept

**VYREON (CARDIA-01)** is Elstella's working concept for a future precision antifibrotic therapy intended to reduce *maladaptive, persistent cardiac fibroblast activity* while preserving the heart's normal tissue-repair functions.

The concept is deliberately state-selective rather than based on indiscriminately suppressing all fibrosis. The central premise is that cardiac fibroblasts are not a single uniform population: their roles vary with disease cause, tissue location, injury stage, and cellular state. A useful therapy may therefore need to distinguish persistent pathological remodeling from necessary repair.

**One-sentence proposition:** Develop a biomarker-informed therapeutic strategy that selectively addresses persistent pathological fibroblast states associated with maladaptive cardiac remodeling, without unnecessarily disrupting normal cardiac repair.

This is a research proposition—not a claim that such selectivity has been achieved.

## 3. The unmet need

Cardiac fibrosis is part of the heart's response to injury and stress. It can help stabilize damaged tissue, but persistent or excessive remodeling can contribute to tissue stiffness, impaired function, and progression of heart disease.

There is no established therapy specifically approved to directly treat myocardial fibrosis. Existing approaches and investigational strategies have not yet solved the translation problem. The field faces important challenges: different diseases produce different fibrotic states, fibroblasts are heterogeneous, timing matters, and results from experimental models do not always predict clinical benefit.

Sources:
- Maitra et al., *Cardiac fibrosis: from mechanisms and models to medicines* (2025): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40877078/
- Maitra et al., *Cardiac Fibrosis as a Dynamic Therapeutic Target* (2026): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42854670/

## 4. Biological rationale

The concept rests on four field-level observations:

1. **Fibroblast heterogeneity:** Cardiac fibroblasts occupy different states and may respond differently to injury, inflammation, mechanical stress, and disease context.
2. **Mechanical signaling:** The physical properties of fibrotic tissue can influence fibroblast behavior.
3. **Disease-stage dependence:** Fibrosis is not a single static process; early repair and later maladaptive remodeling should not be treated as interchangeable.
4. **Translation challenge:** A result in a simplified model is not proof of benefit in people.

A 2025 *Nature* study reported that selective inhibition of stromal mechanosensing, together with modulation of TGF-β signaling, shifted persistently activated cardiac fibroblasts toward a less activated state in experimental systems. This is important prior art. It means VYREON (CARDIA-01) cannot claim that targeting fibroblast mechanosensing or the broad idea of fibroblast-state modulation is new.

Primary study: Cho et al., *Selective inhibition of stromal mechanosensing suppresses cardiac fibrosis* (2025): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40307543/

## 5. VYREON (CARDIA-01)'s proposed differentiation question

**Not:** “Can Elstella invent an antifibrotic drug?”

**Instead:** “Can a therapeutic strategy be designed around a reproducibly identifiable pathological fibroblast state, in a defined disease context and stage, while preserving beneficial repair functions?”

The potential differentiation would have to come from evidence that a specific disease-associated state:
- is reproducibly present in relevant human data;
- can be distinguished from fibroblast states involved in normal repair;
- is associated with a meaningful pathological process;
- offers a sufficiently selective and tractable therapeutic opportunity; and
- can be addressed without unacceptable effects on other cardiac cell types or normal tissue functions.

None of these VYREON (CARDIA-01)-specific conditions has yet been demonstrated.

## 6. Proposed mechanism — deliberately unresolved

The current mechanism is a **therapeutic design objective**, not a confirmed pathway:

- Identify a persistent, disease-associated fibroblast state.
- Determine which biological features distinguish it from healthy and reparative states.
- Establish whether those features are causally relevant rather than merely correlated.
- Evaluate whether a future intervention could selectively modify the pathological state.
- Require evidence that essential cardiac repair and other cell functions are not unacceptably disrupted.

**Target selection is intentionally pending.** Choosing a fashionable target before completing the human-data review would create confirmation bias and could duplicate existing work.

## 7. Biomarker and patient-selection concept

A future precision approach may require a way to identify the relevant disease context and ongoing remodeling. Candidate evidence categories for evaluation include:

- tissue-level measures of fibrosis and remodeling;
- molecular signatures associated with defined fibroblast states;
- disease context and anatomical region;
- indicators of whether remodeling is active or established; and
- measures of cardiac structure and function.

No biomarker panel, diagnostic threshold, or patient-selection rule has been selected or validated.

## 8. What would count as convincing evidence?

VYREON (CARDIA-01) would need to pass separate evidence gates. A positive result at one gate does not automatically satisfy the next.

| Gate | Question that must be answered |
|---|---|
| Human relevance | Is the proposed pathological state supported in independent human datasets? |
| Reproducibility | Does the signal persist across cohorts, regions, and measurement platforms? |
| Biological specificity | Can it be distinguished from normal repair and other cell states? |
| Causality | Is the proposed biology functionally relevant, rather than only correlated? |
| Selectivity | Can the intended effect be separated from unwanted effects on other cells and functions? |
| Safety | Are important risks identified and acceptably addressed in appropriate supervised research? |
| Translational relevance | Do appropriate models represent the human disease context and stage? |
| Clinical value | Is there a credible future way to measure meaningful benefit in people? |

All experimental work must be conducted by qualified researchers in appropriately supervised and approved institutional settings. This concept document does not provide experimental protocols, compound recipes, dosing, or instructions for administering a substance.

## 9. Major risks and failure modes

1. **Biology is too heterogeneous:** no single state or target is sufficiently consistent across the intended population.
2. **Repair is disrupted:** reducing fibroblast activity also interferes with necessary tissue healing.
3. **Weak human relevance:** the strongest signal exists only in a model system.
4. **Poor selectivity:** the intended biology is shared by other cell types or normal functions.
5. **Delivery is not feasible:** a future modality cannot reach the relevant tissue or cell population with adequate selectivity.
6. **Existing work already covers the opportunity:** the proposed differentiation is not supported by a careful literature and intellectual-property review.
7. **Biomarkers are inadequate:** the intended patient subgroup cannot be identified or monitored reliably.

Any of these could justify redesigning or stopping the program.

## 11. Development roadmap — decision gates, not promises

**Phase A — Research definition**
- Complete the CARDIA-NEXUS reproducibility pilot.
- Define the disease context and the biological state under consideration.
- Record contradictory and negative evidence.
- Decide whether a defensible therapeutic question remains.

**Phase B — Target assessment**
- Only after Phase A, assess whether a specific target hypothesis is sufficiently supported.
- Review human relevance, selectivity, biological plausibility, prior art, and potential safety liabilities.
- Obtain independent review from qualified cardiovascular and translational-biology researchers.

**Phase C — Supervised preclinical evaluation**
- If justified, develop a research plan with an appropriate institution or qualified laboratory.
- Use relevant models and predefined measures.
- Evaluate beneficial effects, unintended effects, and reproducibility.
- Do not infer human efficacy from model results.

**Phase D — Translation assessment**
- Reassess whether the evidence supports further development.
- Determine whether manufacturing, formulation, toxicology, regulatory, and clinical-development expertise would be required.
- Advance only if the evidence and governance justify it.

No timeline, cost, clinical milestone, or probability of success is currently supportable.

## 13. The next decision

The immediate decision is **not** to make a compound. It is to determine whether human cardiac data support a sufficiently specific, reproducible, and therapeutically relevant fibroblast-state hypothesis to justify target selection.

**Go:** independent human evidence supports a distinct and potentially actionable biological state, with a credible path to selectivity.

**Revise:** the biology is plausible but the disease context, state definition, or evidence is inconsistent.

**Stop:** the proposed opportunity is already adequately addressed, cannot be reproduced, or lacks a plausible selectivity/safety rationale.
