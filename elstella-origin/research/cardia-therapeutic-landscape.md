# PROJECT CARDIA — THERAPEUTIC LANDSCAPE & TRANSLATIONAL GAP

**Prepared:** 2026-11-24  
**Evidence status:** Rapid literature map; not a systematic review.  
**Purpose:** Define the existing field, avoid rediscovering known work, and identify a defensible research question.  
**Not:** A treatment recommendation, drug candidate, validated target, or claim of Elstella discovery.

## Executive assessment

Cardiac fibrosis is an established disease process with substantial mechanistic research. As of the sources reviewed for this document, no medicine is established specifically to reverse myocardial fibrosis as a standalone indication. The main gap is not simply the absence of molecules: it is the difficulty of translating context-dependent biology into safe, selective, clinically meaningful interventions.

A 2026 review emphasizes disease context, anatomical location, fibroblast state, timing, measurement limitations and trial design as barriers to translation. A 2026 human translational review likewise highlights fibroblast heterogeneity, immune–fibroblast communication, biomarkers and the need to connect fibrosis measurements to patient outcomes. These are literature-supported field observations, not original Elstella findings.

## Therapeutic and mechanistic landscape

| Research area | What the literature supports | Main translational concern | Elstella interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| TGF-β and profibrotic signaling | Established mechanistic pathway implicated in fibroblast activation and matrix production | Broad pathway interference may affect normal repair and other tissues; disease stage matters | Too broad to claim as a differentiated target by itself |
| Neurohormonal / RAAS pathways | Existing cardiovascular therapies can influence remodeling and fibrosis-associated measures | Effects may be indirect and vary by disease context; fibrosis-specific benefit is difficult to isolate | Must separate general cardiac benefit from direct antifibrotic action |
| Fibroblast-state selectivity | Human single-cell and multi-omic studies support distinct fibroblast states and disease-associated trajectories | Markers may not be exclusive; some fibroblast activity is needed for repair and scar integrity | Strong rationale for context-aware evidence mapping, not proof of a safe intervention |
| Immune–fibroblast communication | Human heart multi-omics has identified disease-associated cellular communication patterns | Association and inferred signaling do not automatically establish causality or druggability | Map evidence by cell type, disease stage, tissue and study design |
| Extracellular-matrix remodeling | Matrix deposition, turnover and cross-linking contribute to tissue stiffness and remodeling | Established matrix may be difficult to reverse; surrogate changes may not improve outcomes | Distinguish preventing progression from reversing established fibrosis |
| Cell/gene/immune engineering approaches | Promising proof-of-concept studies exist in preclinical systems | Specificity, delivery, durability, safety, manufacturing and timing remain unresolved | Do not describe preclinical approaches as therapies ready for patients |
| Biomarker-guided treatment | Imaging and circulating biomarkers can help characterize fibrotic burden or activity | Biomarker movement is not equivalent to improved survival, function or quality of life | Require patient-important endpoints in translational assessment |

## Important recent evidence that changes the competitive landscape

### 1. Human heart multi-omics and immune–fibroblast communication

A 2024 *Nature* study profiled 45 healthy donor, acutely infarcted and chronically failing human hearts using multi-omic approaches. It reported disease-associated fibroblast trajectories and distinct populations with features resembling myofibroblasts and matrifibrocytes. This supports biological heterogeneity and the need to preserve disease and cell-state context. It does not, by itself, validate a therapeutic intervention.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08008-5  
PMID: verify from publisher record before entering in the evidence register.

### 2. Translational biology of human cardiac fibrosis

A 2026 *Nature Cardiovascular Research* review synthesizes human single-cell studies, genetic analyses, immune–fibroblast cross-talk and imaging/biomarker approaches. It emphasizes that emerging targeted antifibrotic strategies have shown promise in biomarker measures, while clinical importance remains to be established.

Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44161-026-00891-7

### 3. Cardiac fibrosis as a dynamic therapeutic target

A 2026 *JACC: Basic to Translational Science* review states that no therapy directly targeting myocardial fibrosis is currently established in practice and highlights model limitations, fibroblast heterogeneity, temporal dependence, fibrosis assessment and trial design as translational barriers.

Source: https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacbts.2026.101731  
PMID: 42854670

### 4. A newly reported preclinical discovery: CGS15943

A 2026 *Science* paper reports a multidimensional discovery pipeline using human iPSC-derived systems and identifies CGS15943 as a lead compound in that study. The authors report activity across human cardiac fibroblasts, engineered heart tissues and animal models, with an atypical adenosine-receptor-associated signaling mechanism. This is a major competing result that Elstella must account for. It is not proof of human clinical benefit, and it is not an Elstella discovery.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42784676/  
DOI: 10.1126/science.aej5896

## What this means for Elstella

The generic claim “we will discover a drug that targets cardiac fibrosis” is not differentiated. The field already contains extensive pathway research and new preclinical candidate reports.

A more defensible near-term research question is:

> Can a source-traceable, context-aware evidence framework identify where cardiac-fibrosis therapeutic claims are supported, contradictory, model-dependent or insufficiently translated—and does that map change expert research prioritization compared with a conventional review?

This is a platform/workflow hypothesis. It is testable without pretending a medicine has been discovered.

## Candidate research gaps to test — not established discoveries

1. **Context mixing:** Determine whether pooling HFpEF, post-infarction remodeling, pressure overload and other contexts obscures different cell states or mechanisms.
2. **State and timing:** Determine whether a proposed fibroblast marker or mechanism has consistent meaning across disease stage and tissue location.
3. **Model-to-human distance:** Compare how often promising claims are supported by human tissue, human cell systems, engineered tissues, animal models or only indirect proxies.
4. **Endpoint validity:** Separate changes in collagen markers or imaging surrogates from demonstrated functional or patient-important benefit.
5. **Safety trade-offs:** Record evidence that fibroblast or matrix suppression could interfere with adaptive repair and scar formation.

## Decision

**Do not nominate a therapeutic target or molecule from this rapid map.** First execute the review protocol, verify primary studies, extract contradictory evidence and obtain independent expert review. Only then decide whether one disease context and one biological mechanism merit a supervised experimental research proposal.
