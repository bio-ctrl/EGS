# DIVISION 01 — CELLULAR & MITOCHONDRIAL HEALTH

**Role:** First research division  
**Stage:** Literature-led landscape and feasibility assessment  
**Scope:** High-level scientific research planning and public-data assessment

## Purpose
Build a rigorous understanding of cellular energy biology, mitochondrial function, and the medical needs associated with mitochondrial dysfunction. Determine whether ELSTELLA can make a focused, responsible, evidence-backed contribution.

## Why this is first
Cellular function is foundational to tissue and organ health. Mitochondria are important to cellular energy metabolism and participate in several other cellular processes. This is a scientifically meaningful area for long-term research, but it is broad and competitive. The company must narrow its scope only after understanding the existing evidence and expert landscape.

## Initial work
1. Create a dated literature search strategy and source register.
2. Build a high-level map of cellular energy and mitochondrial biology using authoritative sources.
3. Describe major mitochondrial disease categories and patient needs at an appropriate overview level.
4. Map current care, research programs, clinical-development activity, and established organizations.
5. Inventory relevant public research datasets, access terms, provenance, and limitations.
6. Distinguish established knowledge, preliminary findings, disputed interpretations, and unknowns.
7. Obtain qualified independent scientific critique.
8. Record an advance, revise, or stop decision.

## Boundaries
This charter does not select molecular targets, design or develop drug candidates, specify laboratory experiments, or claim therapeutic efficacy or safety. Any experimental, translational, or clinical research requires qualified institutional leadership, supervision, permissions, and approvals.

## Successful first milestone
A source-traceable landscape report, an auditable public-data inventory, a qualified review record, and a clear decision about whether a narrower research question is justified. A report is not a discovery or a licensing asset.
