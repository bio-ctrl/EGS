# DIVISION 01 — CELLULAR & MITOCHONDRIAL HEALTH: LANDSCAPE FRAMEWORK

**Status:** Research framework; evidence collection and expert review remain outstanding.  
**Purpose:** Establish what is known, what is being investigated, and where credible unmet needs may remain.

## 1. Scope
The first division covers cellular energy biology and mitochondrial health. Its first deliverable is a broad, source-traceable landscape, followed by a narrower scope only if the evidence supports it.

## 2. Questions for the landscape
- What are the established roles of mitochondria in cellular energy metabolism and broader cell function?
- Which disease categories involve mitochondrial dysfunction, and how heterogeneous are they?
- What care and treatment approaches currently exist across relevant conditions?
- Which academic, clinical, nonprofit, and biotechnology groups are active in this field?
- Which findings are supported by human evidence, and which remain preclinical or exploratory?
- Which public datasets are available, under what terms, and with what limitations?
- What questions remain genuinely unresolved after accounting for existing work?
- What expertise and institutional infrastructure would be required to investigate those questions responsibly?

## 3. Evidence classification
Label every record by source type and evidence context: peer-reviewed research, clinical trial registry entry, regulatory information, review article, preprint, or institutional commentary. Distinguish human clinical evidence from animal, cell, computational, or other preclinical work.

## 4. Source register fields
Record title, authors, year, publication or registry, DOI or stable identifier, URL, retrieval date, study type, population or model context, key reported findings, limitations, funding/conflict disclosures when available, and review status.

## 5. Public-data inventory
For each dataset, record its official name, hosting institution, stable link, general data type, species or sample context, access conditions, consent or governance notes when available, documentation quality, known limitations, and suitability for the stated research question. Do not collect or upload identifiable patient information.

## 6. Competitive and institutional landscape
Map relevant research centers, academic groups, nonprofit organizations, biotechnology companies, and clinical-development activity using public information. This is to understand the field and identify potential expert feedback—not to imply partnership or claim novelty.

## 7. Decision criteria
Proceed only if the medical need is meaningful, existing research has been mapped, the question is narrow and answerable, data access and limitations are understood, qualified reviewers consider the question coherent, and the company has a realistic capability and institutional pathway.

## 8. Deliverables
1. Dated literature and source register.
2. High-level scientific overview.
3. Existing-care and research landscape.
4. Public dataset inventory.
5. Evidence gaps, uncertainty, and feasibility assessment.
6. Qualified review record.
7. Written advance, revise, or stop decision.

This framework does not specify drug targets, candidate molecules, experimental protocols, or therapeutic claims.
