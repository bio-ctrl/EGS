# PROJECT GENESIS — Initial Research Charter

## Decision
Begin with a literature and public-dataset feasibility audit. Do not begin with a claim of discovery or a plan to alter DNA.

## Primary question
What reproducible relationships between developmental gene-regulatory programs and biological form are already established across published comparative developmental studies, and where do meaningful evidence gaps remain?

## Work packages
1. Literature inventory: Evo-Devo, gene regulation, morphogenesis, developmental timing, cell differentiation.
2. Dataset inventory: published public developmental datasets, specimen-level metadata, assay, stage, tissue, species, and access conditions.
3. Evidence classification: direct observation, source-reported experimental evidence, computational inference, review-level synthesis.
4. Gap assessment: compare the proposed scope with existing atlases, reviews, databases, and analysis tools.
5. Research decision: advance only if a narrow, testable question remains and suitable independent data exist.

## Minimum quality controls
- Every claim has a source.
- Every dataset has an accession or stable source URL.
- Species, tissue, developmental stage, assay, and specimen count are recorded when available.
- Reused cohorts are identified; reused samples are not called independent replication.
- Cells are not treated as independent organisms.
- Missing values remain explicitly missing; they are not guessed.
- Negative and contradictory evidence is retained.
- No causal language is used for descriptive associations.

## Gate criteria

### Advance
A narrow research gap remains after comparison with prior work, and at least two suitable independent datasets or a clearly justified single-dataset methods question are available.

### Revise
The topic is scientifically interesting but too broad, datasets are heterogeneous, or metadata are incomplete.

### Stop
Existing work already answers the question, no suitable data exist, or the proposed analysis cannot add reproducible value.

## Boundaries
This charter covers literature research, metadata curation, and descriptive analysis of published data. It does not authorize or describe DNA design, gene editing, organism engineering, wet-lab experiments, or human/animal research. Such work requires qualified institutional oversight and applicable approvals.

## Reporting language
Use “proposed,” “under investigation,” “reported in the source study,” and “not established” accurately. Do not use “discovered,” “novel,” “validated,” “effective,” or “therapeutic” unless supported by appropriate evidence.
