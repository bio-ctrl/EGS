# PROJECT LEIGH — INITIAL RESEARCH CHARTER

**Program status:** Initial literature-led research assessment  
**Disease area:** Leigh syndrome spectrum (LSS)  
**Owner:** ELSTELLA & CO.  
**Decision status:** Proposed focus; not an approved institutional research protocol

## 1. Purpose

Establish a rigorous, source-traceable understanding of LSS, its current care and research landscape, and the evidence gaps that may warrant further qualified investigation.

The long-term ambition is to contribute to better treatments and, ultimately, a cure. This charter does not claim that Elstella has discovered a treatment, identified a therapeutic target, or established a drug-development program.

## 2. Why this focus requires discipline

LSS is genetically and clinically heterogeneous. The disease spectrum cannot be treated as one uniform biological problem. Research published in 2026 includes computational screening and human brain organoid work reporting preclinical findings, while international natural-history research is improving understanding of disease progression and outcome measures.

These developments mean Elstella must map the field accurately before describing any gap as novel or underserved.

## 3. Core assessment questions

1. How is LSS currently defined, diagnosed, and characterized in authoritative sources?
2. What are the major dimensions of genetic and clinical heterogeneity?
3. What care and treatment approaches are described in current clinical references?
4. What therapeutic research and clinical-development activity is already public?
5. What outcomes matter to patients, families, and clinicians according to credible published sources?
6. What public datasets and research resources are available, and under what terms?
7. Where are findings preliminary, contradictory, poorly characterized, or not generalizable?
8. Is there a narrow, feasible research question that Elstella could responsibly investigate with qualified review?

## 4. Work scope

Permitted initial work:
- Literature searching, reading, and source verification.
- Structured summaries with citations and publication dates.
- High-level mapping of disease context and research activity.
- Metadata inventory of public datasets and their access terms.
- Evidence-quality and uncertainty documentation.
- Software prototyping for literature organization and research provenance.
- Independent critique by qualified scientific mentors.

Out of scope:
- Designing, selecting, or optimizing drug candidates, targets, or therapeutic interventions.
- Experimental protocols or wet-lab work.
- Human or animal subject research, sample collection, or clinical activity.
- Claims of efficacy, safety, cure, novelty, patentability, or clinical utility.
- Any activity requiring institutional oversight without a qualified institutional partner and formal approvals.

## 5. Deliverables

1. Dated literature search log and source register.
2. Disease-context and patient-need summary.
3. Current care and treatment landscape.
4. Research and clinical-development landscape.
5. Public-data inventory with provenance, terms, and limitations.
6. Evidence-gap and feasibility matrix.
7. Independent reviewer comments and responses.
8. Written advance, revise, or stop recommendation.

## 6. Evidence standards

Every material statement must be linked to a source. Record publication date, source type, population or model context, limitations, and whether the claim is directly reported or interpreted. Distinguish peer-reviewed research, clinical trial records, preprints, reviews, institutional pages, and advocacy material.

Preclinical findings must never be described as proof of clinical effectiveness. An absence of evidence in a search is not proof that no research exists.

## 7. Decision gates

- **Advance:** A qualified reviewer agrees that the landscape supports a specific, useful, and feasible next research question.
- **Revise:** The question is too broad, overlaps existing work, or lacks suitable data.
- **Stop:** The proposed contribution is not differentiated, feasible, or supported by evidence.

## 8. Governance

Any future experimental or translational work requires qualified institutional partners, experienced supervision, appropriate permissions, training, and regulatory review. Because the founder is a minor, formal company, financing, IP, and institutional matters require parent/guardian involvement and qualified professional guidance.

**Founding rule:** Evidence before claims.
