# PROJECT LEIGH — SCIENTIFIC LANDSCAPE ASSESSMENT 001

**Prepared:** 24 November 2026  
**Status:** Initial desk-research baseline; not independently reviewed  
**Purpose:** Establish what is already known and define what must be verified next.

## Executive assessment

Leigh syndrome spectrum (LSS) is a serious, genetically heterogeneous mitochondrial disease spectrum. It remains an area of substantial medical need, but it is not an empty research field. Published reviews, ongoing natural-history work, and recent preclinical research show active investigation.

The strongest immediate opportunity for Elstella is not to announce a discovery or claim a treatment gap. It is to produce a careful, current, source-traceable map of the field and test whether its Research Atlas makes that work more reliable or efficient.

**Current recommendation:** Proceed with the literature and evidence-mapping assessment only. Do not claim a therapeutic discovery, proprietary target, candidate, or licensing asset.

## 1. Disease context

LSS is associated with dysfunction in mitochondrial energy biology and can involve genetic causes in mitochondrial DNA or nuclear DNA. Clinical presentation and disease progression vary. This heterogeneity complicates attempts to identify a universal treatment strategy.

A 2025 review summarizes the genetic and clinical diversity and describes the absence of a cure at the time of publication:
- Magro et al., *Leigh Syndrome: A Comprehensive Review of the Disease and Present and Future Treatments*, 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40149709/

A 2026 natural-history publication describes an international multi-site observational effort and emphasizes the importance of better characterizing progression and outcome measures:
- MacMullen et al., *Interim analysis of an international multi-site prospective natural history study evaluating the clinical presentation and progression of Leigh syndrome spectrum disorders*, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1177/26330040261476231

## 2. Existing research — important competitive context

A 2026 *Nature Communications* paper reports a computational screening approach combined with human brain organoid models. The authors report preclinical findings involving neuronal morphogenesis and organoid measures. This is significant evidence of active work in the field.

Source:
- Menacho et al., *Accelerating Leigh syndrome drug discovery through deep learning screening in brain organoids*, 20 April 2026. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-71391-2

**Interpretation boundary:** The paper reports preclinical research. It does not establish clinical effectiveness or a cure. Elstella must not present the approach, compounds, or findings as its own work or imply that it has independently reproduced them.

A University of Luxembourg institutional summary also describes the collaboration and research context:
- University of Luxembourg, *Accelerating drug discovery for Leigh syndrome*, 20 April 2026. https://www.uni.lu/lcsb-en/news/accelerating-drug-discovery-for-leigh-syndrome/

## 3. What this means for Elstella

The presence of recent computational and model-based research makes generic claims such as "AI has not been applied to Leigh syndrome" indefensible.

Elstella must answer narrower questions:
- Can it create a more traceable and useful evidence map than an ordinary literature review?
- Can it reliably distinguish model-specific preclinical results from human clinical evidence?
- Can it identify missing context, contradictions, and data limitations without overstating conclusions?
- Can qualified reviewers verify that the platform saves time or improves completeness?

These are platform-validation questions, not therapeutic discoveries.

## 4. Initial evidence gaps to investigate

The following are assessment topics, not established discoveries by Elstella:

1. How published clinical and natural-history outcomes are defined and compared across cohorts.
2. How genetic and clinical heterogeneity is represented in reviews, datasets, and trial records.
3. Which public datasets are actually accessible, sufficiently documented, and appropriate for descriptive research.
4. Where preclinical findings have or have not been followed by independent replication or human studies.
5. How patient- and family-prioritized outcomes are represented in published research.

Each item requires source verification and qualified scientific review before being described as a genuine gap.

## 5. Research Atlas validation

The platform should be tested on a defined literature-mapping task against a conventional manual workflow. Record:
- Search coverage and source traceability.
- Accuracy of bibliographic metadata.
- Correct classification of study type and model context.
- Ability to preserve limitations and uncertainty.
- Time required for the same task.
- Reviewer corrections and failure cases.

Do not use automated summaries as authoritative evidence. Human verification remains necessary.

## 6. Risks and limitations

- This is an initial desk-research baseline, not a systematic review.
- Search coverage is incomplete and must be documented.
- Clinical trial records and treatment status change; verify primary registries and authoritative clinical sources before presenting a current status.
- Published preclinical findings may not translate to human benefit.
- Dataset access terms, privacy conditions, and suitability must be reviewed before use.
- No therapeutic hypothesis, target, candidate, patent position, or licensing opportunity has been validated.
