# PROJECT MITOS — INITIAL RESEARCH CHARTER

**Program:** Mitochondrial & Cellular Energy Medicine  
**Role:** Elstella's initial research priority  
**Stage:** Literature-led research planning  
**Owner:** ELSTELLA & CO.  
**Status:** Proposed; not a validated therapeutic program

## 1. Purpose

PROJECT MITOS will assess whether a specific, meaningful unmet medical need exists within mitochondrial disease and cellular energy disorders, and whether a narrow research question can be investigated responsibly using published evidence and lawfully accessible public data.

The project does not begin with a presumed drug, target, mechanism, or treatment. It begins by examining disease burden, existing care, scientific evidence, competing development programs, and research feasibility.

## 2. Central question

Which well-defined mitochondrial disease context has a clinically meaningful unmet need, a sufficiently understood evidence base, and a feasible initial research question that merits further investigation by a qualified team?

This question is intentionally broad at the charter stage. It must be narrowed only after the landscape assessment.

## 3. Scope of the initial phase

Permitted initial work:
- Literature search and source screening.
- Summaries of disease categories, clinical context, and existing standard-of-care options from credible sources.
- High-level review of approved treatments and publicly documented clinical-development activity.
- Competitor and prior-art landscape mapping.
- Inventory of lawfully accessible public datasets and their metadata.
- Descriptive comparison of published findings, including uncertainty and limitations.
- Written research-gap and feasibility assessment.

The initial phase is desk research and safe computational work only.

## 4. Out of scope

This charter does not authorize or describe:
- Designing, selecting, synthesizing, or optimizing drug molecules or therapeutic candidates.
- Selecting actionable molecular targets or creating intervention protocols.
- Wet-lab experiments, biological sample handling, animal work, or human-subject research.
- Genetic modification, gene editing, organism engineering, or biological trait alteration.
- Diagnosis, treatment recommendations, or claims of clinical benefit.

Any future experimental or translational research would require qualified institutional partners, experienced supervision, appropriate training, permissions, approvals, and regulatory oversight.

## 5. Evidence standards

For each material claim, record:
- Full citation and stable identifier.
- Publication type and date.
- Disease population and context.
- Study design and model system.
- Outcomes actually reported.
- Whether evidence is human, animal, in vitro, computational, review-level, or guidance.
- Limitations, uncertainty, conflicts, and replication status.
- Whether the statement is a direct study result, author interpretation, or Elstella synthesis.

Do not treat mechanistic plausibility as proof of therapeutic effect. Do not treat a preclinical result as clinical efficacy.

## 6. Initial deliverables

1. A dated literature search log.
2. A source register with screening decisions.
3. A summary of existing treatments and unmet needs.
4. A high-level competitor and clinical-development landscape.
5. A public dataset inventory with access and quality notes.
6. A disease-area comparison and research-gap assessment.
7. A recommendation to advance, revise, or stop.

## 7. Advance / revise / stop criteria

**Advance** only if the review identifies a specific, important unmet need; the evidence supports a clear and falsifiable research question; suitable data or a qualified institutional pathway exists; and independent reviewers agree that the question is worth pursuing.

**Revise** if the medical need is meaningful but the question is too broad, the evidence is mixed, or the data are not sufficiently comparable.

**Stop** if the gap is not substantiated, existing treatments or development programs already address the proposed need adequately, the evidence base cannot support a useful question, or the project would require resources and expertise that are not realistically available.

## 8. Current status statement

No disease indication has been selected as the lead indication. No drug candidate, molecular target, therapeutic mechanism, efficacy, safety, or clinical benefit has been established. This charter is a research-planning document, not evidence that a discovery has occurred.
