# PROJECT MITOS — UNMET-NEED & LANDSCAPE ASSESSMENT

**Purpose:** Determine whether the initial mitochondrial-medicine research direction is sufficiently important, differentiated, and feasible to justify further work.  
**Status:** Assessment framework; research not yet completed.

## 1. Decision to be made

Should Elstella continue investigating a narrowly defined mitochondrial disease context, revise the scope, or stop?

Do not choose a lead indication based on market size, novelty claims, or a single promising paper. The decision must consider medical need, current care, scientific maturity, competition, data access, and feasibility together.

## 2. Assessment dimensions

### A. Medical need
- What condition and patient population are being considered?
- What is the burden and severity of the condition?
- Which outcomes matter to patients and clinicians?
- What limitations remain in current diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment?
- Are there meaningful differences among disease subtypes?

### B. Current treatment landscape
- What treatments are approved for the specific condition and jurisdiction?
- What are their established indications and limitations?
- What is standard supportive care?
- What evidence supports the reported benefits and risks?
- Which needs remain unmet despite existing care?

Use regulator labels, clinical guidelines, systematic reviews, and primary studies where available. Do not infer treatment effectiveness from marketing material.

### C. Competitive and development landscape
- Which pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies are active in the area?
- Which academic groups and consortia publish relevant work?
- What clinical trials or public development programs are registered?
- What approaches have failed, been discontinued, or produced inconclusive results?
- What intellectual-property or freedom-to-operate questions require professional review?

An apparent lack of competitors is not automatically attractive; it may indicate scientific difficulty, weak commercial incentives, or an inadequate evidence base.

### D. Biological evidence
- What biological features are consistently reported?
- Which findings are replicated?
- Which claims are based only on models or computational inference?
- Where do studies disagree?
- What evidence is missing to establish causality or clinical relevance?

This assessment remains descriptive. It does not nominate drug targets or interventions.

### E. Data feasibility
- Are public datasets available for the disease context?
- Are sample sizes, metadata, tissue/source, and outcome definitions adequate?
- Are access terms and permitted uses clear?
- Are cohorts independent or overlapping?
- Can an analysis be reproduced without patient-identifiable information?

### F. Commercial and execution feasibility
- Is the patient need sufficiently important?
- Is there a plausible future market and development pathway?
- What specialized expertise and institutional partners would be required?
- What are the major cost, time, regulatory, and reimbursement uncertainties?
- Is the opportunity realistic for an early-stage team to investigate at the current stage?

## 3. Required evidence register fields

Use the companion CSV template to record:
- Source ID and full citation.
- Source type, date, and stable identifier.
- Disease and population.
- Claim or result relevant to the assessment.
- Evidence class and study design.
- Limitations and conflicts.
- Screening decision and reason.
- Reviewer and review date.

## 4. Disease-area comparison

Compare candidate disease contexts using qualitative ratings supported by written evidence:
- Unmet medical need.
- Limitations of current treatment.
- Strength and consistency of biological evidence.
- Availability and quality of public data.
- Competitive intensity and development activity.
- Feasibility of a narrow initial research question.
- Need for specialist or institutional resources.

Do not convert unsupported assumptions into numerical scores. Record unknowns explicitly.

## 5. Required output

The final assessment must include:
1. Search methods and dates.
2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria.
3. Summary of current care.
4. Competitor and clinical-development map.
5. Evidence-strength and uncertainty summary.
6. Data availability and quality review.
7. Risks, assumptions, and unanswered questions.
8. A clear advance, revise, or stop recommendation.

## 6. Limitations

This document is a review framework, not a completed systematic review, market study, clinical assessment, or investment analysis. It makes no claim that a particular mitochondrial disease is underserved, commercially attractive, or scientifically tractable until the review is completed.
